Lectures

01: Introduction

Monday, 12 October 2026, 13:45–15:15, Hörsaal 9.122

Overview

The opening lecture explains how the course works and then asks where the “thinking” of a language model comes from, and how it differs from human thinking. It starts with what AI does today (protein structure prediction, the automation of text and code, the growing length of programming tasks AI can complete) and with how a model condenses its training data into representations instead of memorising it. It then looks at what shapes human language and thought: perception, the physical environment and culture, with examples from spatial language, the Aymara placing of the past in front of the speaker, and colour terms across languages. The lecture ends by comparing the conditions under which humans and models learn, with a short pointer to the lecturer’s research on embodiment and spatial schemas in language and vision models.

Topics

  • Course structure: 12 lectures, 3 practical seminars, 1 exam; selection process; materials on this website
  • AI vs. human intelligence today: AlphaFold, automation of text and code, length of tasks AI can solve
  • Where machine “thinking” comes from: knowledge has to be represented, not memorised
  • What shapes human language: perception, environment, culture, and cultural bias in LLMs
  • How language shapes thought: figure and ground, Aymara time gestures, colour terms
  • Human and machine learning conditions compared
  • Research pointers: embodiment in figurative language, spatial schemas in language and vision models
