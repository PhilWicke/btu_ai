03 — NLP Fundamentals

Monday, 27 April 2026 — Hörsaal 9.122

Overview

This lecture covers the foundations of Natural Language Processing and bridges the gap from classical text representations to modern language models. We explore why building a model of human language matters, how distributional semantics captures word meaning through context, and how word embeddings (word2vec) encode semantic relationships as vectors. The lecture concludes with a first look at language models, masked language modeling (BERT), and the transformer architecture.

Topics

  • What is NLP? Sentiment analysis, NLU, topic modelling, machine translation
  • Why build a model of human language? From task-specific models to foundation models
  • NLP as a foundation for robotics and multimodal AI
  • Distributional semantics: “a word is characterized by the company it keeps” (Firth, 1957)
  • N-grams and context windows
  • Word2vec: from co-occurrence to dense vector representations
  • Cosine similarity and vector arithmetic (king − man + woman = queen)
  • Cross-lingual word embeddings
  • Limitations of static word embeddings (polysemy)
  • From word embeddings to language models: encoder-decoder, masked language modeling (BERT)
  • RNNs vs. transformers: positional encoding, self-attention, parallelization
