06: Pretraining and Scaling

Monday, 15 June 2026, Hörsaal 9.122

Overview

The transformer block branches into three families: encoder (BERT), decoder (GPT), encoder-decoder (T5). Each comes with its own pretraining objective: masked language modelling, causal language modelling, span corruption. The lecture works through each objective, shows how the same block becomes a different model under each training signal, and follows the 2018–2020 paradigm of “pretrain once, fine-tune many times.” The story ends with the shift from fine-tuning to in-context learning as models scale: GPT-2 zero-shot, GPT-3 few-shot, no gradient updates at inference.

Topics

  • Three architecture families: encoder, decoder, encoder-decoder
  • The 2018 ImageNet moment: pretrain once, fine-tune everywhere
  • Causal language modelling: every position in every sentence is a free training example
  • Why bidirectional next-word prediction collapses, and how BERT fixes it
  • Masked language modelling and the 80/10/10 substitution rule
  • Two meanings of "mask": BERT masks the data, GPT masks the attention
  • Fine-tuning BERT (new head per task) vs. fine-tuning GPT (target text changes)
  • T5 and span corruption: every task as text-to-text
  • GPT-1 (117M), GPT-2 (1.5B), GPT-3 (175B): the scaling trajectory
  • From fine-tune per task, to zero-shot, to few-shot in-context
  • Scaling laws (Kaplan, Chinchilla) and the question of emergence
