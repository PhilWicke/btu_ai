07: Alignment and Reasoning

Monday, 22 June 2026, Hörsaal 9.122

Overview

A base model knows the world but autocompletes questions instead of answering them. Three training stages turn it into an assistant: supervised fine-tuning on demonstrations, RLHF on human preference, and reinforcement learning against verifiable rewards. The lecture walks each stage, the failure modes that show up at every step (reward hacking, Goodhart drift, sycophancy), and ends at the inference-time scaling axis opened by o1 and DeepSeek-R1: more tokens, more thinking, more compute spent at runtime.

Topics

  • Base vs chat model: identical knowledge, different response policy
  • Helpful, harmless, honest as the assistant target
  • Supervised fine-tuning: InstructGPT (13k demonstrations), LIMA (1k)
  • The superficial alignment hypothesis and what it does and does not claim
  • Reward models, Bradley-Terry, and where the preference signal comes from
  • PPO, the RLHF loop, and the KL leash that keeps the policy on-distribution
  • Reward hacking: Coast Runners, Goodhart's law, sycophancy (the April 2025 GPT-4o rollback)
  • DPO: optimising directly on preference pairs, no reward model needed
  • Chain-of-thought prompting, "let's think step by step," self-consistency
  • Kahneman's two systems: a forward pass is System 1; the scratchpad buys System 2
  • Are chains of thought the real reasoning? Useful computation, imperfect testimony
  • Verifiable rewards: maths and code as graders, no KL leash needed
  • o1 and inference-time scaling: a second axis on top of training compute
  • DeepSeek-R1 and GRPO: long-chain reasoning from pure RL on a base model
